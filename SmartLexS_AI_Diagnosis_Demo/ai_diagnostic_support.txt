Title: AI Diagnostic Support in Clinical Practice

Artificial intelligence can support clinical diagnosis by identifying patterns in medical images, laboratory results, and patient histories. A diagnostic support system may compare a patient's findings with patterns learned from carefully curated clinical datasets and highlight possible conditions for a clinician to review.

For example, an AI model trained on chest X-rays may flag image features associated with pneumonia. The model's output is not a final diagnosis: clinicians must consider symptoms, examination findings, medical history, and other tests before deciding on treatment.

AI tools can improve consistency and help clinicians prioritize cases, especially when workloads are high. Their performance depends on data quality, representative training examples, external validation, and appropriate monitoring after deployment. A system may perform less reliably when used with patient groups or imaging equipment that differ from its training data.

The safest use of AI is as decision support rather than a replacement for professional judgment. Clinical teams should be able to inspect relevant evidence, understand known limitations, and override recommendations when the available evidence supports a different conclusion.
